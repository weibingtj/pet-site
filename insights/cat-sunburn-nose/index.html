Cat Sunburn on the Nose: Signs, Treatment and Prevention

Skin & Coat · 2026-10-31

Bottom line: Keep pale-nosed cats out of strong sun, treat mild burns with cool compresses, and have any sore on the nose that fails to heal in two weeks checked by a vet.

White and pale-colored cats can get a genuine sunburn on the nose and ears, often after a single long afternoon in strong sun. The first signs are redness and fine peeling on the nose bridge or ear edges, and the real risk is not the burn itself but repeated UV damage over the years, which can progress to precancerous lesions and skin cancer in susceptible cats. The fix is simple and cheap: manage sun exposure early.

Why white cats burn and other cats do not

Melanin is natural sunscreen. Cats with white or very pale fur, especially those with pink noses and pale ear tips, have little pigment in exactly the places where fur is thinnest. The nose, ear margins, and eyelids take the full UV load. Sunburn shows up fastest in summer, but it is not purely an outdoor-cat problem: UVA rays pass through window glass, so a white cat that sunbathes in a sunny window for hours daily can burn gradually indoors too.

What sunburn looks like on a cat

Mild to moderate sunburn in cats typically includes:

  • Pink or red skin on the nose bridge, ear edges, or eyelids
  • Dry, flaky, or peeling skin a day or two after exposure
  • Hair thinning on the outer ear tips
  • Curling or thickening of ear edges after repeated burns
  • Crusty or scabby sores that come and go in the same spot

A mild burn may just look like a blush on the nose. More serious damage shows as crusting, open sores, or a sore that bleeds, scabs, and reopens in the same place for weeks.

What to do for a mild sunburn

If the skin is pink and flaky but unbroken:

1. Move your cat out of the sun immediately and keep them shaded or indoors for one to two weeks while the skin recovers.

2. Apply a cool, damp compress for a few minutes, two or three times a day, to ease the sting.

3. Skip human products entirely. Sunscreen designed for people, aloe gels, and moisturizers get licked off, and zinc oxide is toxic to cats if swallowed. Nothing that goes on a cat's nose should assume it will stay there.

4. Keep the environment calm. A stinging nose makes some cats grumpy; leave the handling to soft meals and quiet company.

The skin usually settles within a week once exposure stops.

When it is more than sunburn

Repeated sun damage in white cats can produce actinic keratosis, a precancerous roughness and crusting on the nose and ears, which can advance to squamous cell carcinoma, the most common skin cancer of the cat nose. Warning signs that a sore has moved past simple sunburn:

  • A scab or ulcer on the nose or ear edge that has not healed after two weeks
  • A sore that bleeds, shrinks, and reopens in the same spot
  • Raised, irregular, or raw-looking tissue
  • The nose tip changing shape or the ear edge curling and thickening

These changes are very treatable when caught early, often with surgery, cryotherapy, or topical treatment, and much harder to treat once advanced. This is the reason a persistent nose sore deserves a vet visit rather than another wait-and-see week. For broader guidance on coat and skin problems, see our skin and coat guide.

Preventing sunburn in cats

Prevention is where you win:

  • Limit direct sun between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., when UV is strongest. Indoors during those hours is fine with shade available.
  • Use UV-filtering film on favorite window perches. It cuts the UVA that passes through glass and does nothing to spoil the view.
  • Provide shade outdoors: shaded runs, catios with covers, and leafy shelter rather than open rooftops.
  • Ask your vet about pet-safe sunscreen for high-risk cats. Products made for animals go on the nose and ear tips before sun exposure and are formulated to be licked off in small amounts. Reapply as directed, since grooming removes it quickly. Pet-safe sunscreen and other outdoor essentials are available in our shop.
  • Consider lightweight sun shirts for hairless breeds such as Sphynx, which burn faster than any other cat.

When to see a vet

Book a vet visit for any sore on a cat's nose or ears that has not healed within two weeks, bleeds repeatedly, or looks thickened and raw rather than flaky. Also seek care if a burn looks painful, the nose is swollen, or your cat stops eating. Early assessment of a non-healing sore matters enormously with sun-related skin cancer, and a simple exam or small biopsy gives you a clear answer.

The bottom line

Keep pale-nosed cats out of strong sun, treat mild burns with cool compresses, and have any sore on the nose that fails to heal in two weeks checked by a vet.

Sources: VCA Animal Hospitals: Sunburn in Pets; Cornell University College of Veterinary Medicine: Skin Cancer (Squamous Cell Carcinoma) in Cats.

FAQs

Can indoor cats get sunburned?

Yes. UVA rays pass through window glass, so a white cat that lies in a sunny window for hours every day can develop sun damage on the nose and ear tips over time.

Can I put sunscreen on my cat's nose?

Only products formulated for pets. Human sunscreen often contains zinc oxide, which is toxic to cats when licked off. Ask your vet to recommend a pet-safe option and apply it to the nose and ear tips before sun exposure.

What does cat skin cancer on the nose look like?

Typically a crusted, non-healing sore or ulcer on the nose or ear edge that bleeds and reopens in the same spot. Any such sore lasting more than two weeks should be examined by a vet.

Does cat sunburn heal on its own?

Mild sunburn settles within about a week once the cat stays out of the sun. Repeated burns, however, cause cumulative damage, so prevention matters more than treating each burn.
