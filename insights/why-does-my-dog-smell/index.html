Why Does My Dog Smell? 6 Causes and How to Fix It Fast

Grooming, Paws & Ears · 2026-10-08

Normal dog smell versus problem odour

Every dog has a scent. A mild, warm "doggy" smell that gets stronger when the coat is wet is normal, and it fades as the coat dries. Problem odour is different in three ways:

  • It comes back fast - within a day or two of a bath.
  • It has a direction - yeasty and musty, sour, fishy, rotten, or like corn chips.
  • It has company - redness, head shaking, scratching, greasy fur, hair loss, or scooting.

If you can describe the smell in one word, you have usually narrowed the source to one or two of the six causes below.

1. Ears - yeast or bacteria

The classic ear smell is musty, sour or faintly like corn chips, and it usually comes with head shaking, scratching at one ear, or brown debris in the canal. Floppy-eared breeds such as Cocker Spaniels, Basset Hounds and Golden Retrievers are the most prone, because a covered canal stays warm and damp. Cleaning helps hygiene, but a recurring smell usually means an infection is still active - our dog ear infection guide walks through the signs. Use a pet ear cleaning liquid, never a cotton bud, and see our grooming, paws and ears guide for the safe technique.

2. Skin - seborrhoea, yeast or allergies

Skin odour is greasy, waxy or sour, and you notice it most over the back, along the tail base, or under the belly. Yeast overgrowth smells distinctly musty; bacterial skin infection can smell sharp and rotten. Look for dandruff, greasy patches, redness between the toes, or dark thickened skin. Allergies are the most common driver, and they need diagnosis rather than more shampoo - the skin and coat guide covers the difference between a hygiene problem and a medical one.

3. Mouth and teeth

Rotten or metallic breath is dental disease until proven otherwise. By age three most dogs already have some periodontal disease, and the bacteria living under the gumline produce the sulphur compounds behind that smell. Drooling, dropping food, or chewing on one side means it has gone past odour into pain. Daily or near-daily oral care slows it down; nothing at home removes tartar that has already formed. 4. Anal glands

A sudden fishy or metallic smell, often in one spot where your dog sat, is the anal gland signature. Add scooting, licking under the tail, or straining and the picture is clear. Glands may need expressing, or may be impacted or infected - which is a vet job, not a home job. Our anal gland and scooting guide explains what is normal and when to stop trying at home.

5. Coat, moisture and trapped grime

Wet dog smell is not just water. When the undercoat stays damp, bacteria and yeast multiply in the warm layer next to the skin, and the smell lingers long after the surface looks dry. Double-coated breeds, dogs who swim, and dogs who wear a harness all day are the usual suspects. The fix is thorough drying, especially under the collar, in the armpits and around the tail, plus regular brushing to clear dead undercoat that holds moisture.

6. Environment and grooming habits

Sometimes the dog is fine and the smell is not the dog: musty bedding, a damp collar or harness, a car seat cover, old hair in the vacuum, or a litter of shed fur behind the sofa. The opposite mistake also causes odour. Bathing too often strips the skin's oils, the skin overproduces to compensate, and a greasy, smelly coat follows within days.

A 7-day reset plan

1. Day 1 - Bath properly. Use a pH-balanced pet shampoo and rinse until the water runs clear. Human shampoo has the wrong pH for dog skin.

2. Day 1 - Dry completely. Towel, then air or a cool dryer until you can part the coat and feel dry skin everywhere.

3. Day 1 - Wash everything the dog touches. Bedding, collar, harness, lead, toys.

4. Day 2 - Check the ears. Smell each one. Clean only if there is wax or odour, using a pet ear cleaner.

5. Day 3 - Brush out dead undercoat. This is where much of the trapped moisture and smell lives.

6. Day 4 - Look at the mouth. Lift the lip and check the back teeth and gumline on both sides.

7. Day 5 - Check the paws. Yeast between the pads smells like corn chips and is easy to miss. See the paw care section for what to look for.

8. Day 7 - Judge the result. No smell, or smell returning only after a muddy walk, means you solved a hygiene problem. Smell back within 48 hours means the cause is medical.

Products that help - and what they should not hide

  • A pH-balanced pet shampoo and conditioner for the routine bath.
  • A pet ear cleaning liquid for wax, used only when there is something to clean.
  • A waterless foam or grooming wipe for spot cleaning between baths.
  • A fabric-safe deodoriser for bedding (helpful) - and a note that perfumed sprays on the dog itself only mask a smell that will return.

Do not over-bathe. Most healthy dogs do well with a bath every 4 to 6 weeks, with waterless freshening in between. Medicated or prescription shampoos should only be used on your vet's instruction, because the wrong one can worsen the skin or interfere with later testing.

When to see a vet

See a vet if the odour returns within 48 hours of a proper bath, if it has a fishy component with scooting, if your dog shakes its head or the ear is tender, if the skin is red, greasy, thickened or losing hair, or if the breath is rotten with drooling or dropping food. Those patterns point to infection, allergies, parasites or impacted anal glands that need diagnosis and usually prescription treatment - shampoo alone will not fix them. Also see a vet if a dog who never smelled bad suddenly does, because a new odour in an older dog can flag an internal problem. This article is general education, not veterinary advice.

FAQs

How often is it safe to bathe a smelly dog?

For most healthy dogs, every 4 to 6 weeks is plenty, with waterless foam or wipes in between. Bathing more often than weekly strips the skin's oils and can cause a rebound greasy coat that smells worse. If you feel you must bathe weekly, the cause is likely medical.

Do dog deodorising sprays actually work?

They mask odour for a few hours. On a dog with healthy skin and a muddy afternoon, that is fine. If a spray is the only way your dog smells acceptable, the underlying cause - ears, skin, teeth or glands - is still there and should be diagnosed.

My dog smells musty even after a bath. What is going on?

A musty smell that survives a bath usually points to yeast in the ears or on the skin. Over-the-counter grooming will not clear it. Your vet can confirm with a swab or tape impression and prescribe the right treatment.

Is wet dog smell a sign of anything serious?

On its own, no - it is bacteria and yeast in a damp undercoat. It becomes a flag when it lingers once the coat is fully dry, when the skin underneath is red or greasy, or when it appears in a dog who has never had it before.
