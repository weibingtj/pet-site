How to Clean Cat Ears Safely: A Calm Step-by-Step Method

Grooming, Paws & Ears · 2026-10-11

Most healthy cats need almost nothing done to their ears - a monthly glance and a wipe of the visible flap with a damp cotton pad is plenty, and anything more aggressive usually causes the problem it was meant to prevent. Clean only when you can see debris, use a vet-approved ear cleaner rather than water or peroxide, and never push anything down the canal. If an ear smells, hurts, or your cat is shaking and scratching, that ear needs an examination before it needs a clean.

Do cat ears even need cleaning?

Usually, no. Cats groom their own ears as part of their daily routine, and the ear canal is self-cleaning, with wax migrating slowly outwards. A cat that is healthy, parasite-free and eating well generally keeps its ears in good shape without help.

Cleaning becomes useful in three situations:

1. Your vet has prescribed it as part of treating an infection, allergy or mite infestation.

2. You can see visible debris on the flap or just inside the opening - brown specks, greasy build-up, or dried discharge.

3. Breed or age makes self-grooming harder - older cats with arthritis, overweight cats, or cats with folds around the ear opening.

Outside of those, over-cleaning is the more common mistake. Frequent wiping strips the natural oils and microflora that keep the canal healthy, and it irritates skin that was fine.

How to clean a cat's ears, step by step

Work in a bright room, have everything within reach, and pick a moment when your cat is relaxed after a meal. Two people makes it much easier.

1. Get the right product. A veterinary ear cleaning solution made for cats. Never use water alone, hydrogen peroxide, alcohol, vinegar or human ear drops - these can irritate or damage the canal, and peroxide in particular should never go into an ear.

2. Warm the bottle in your hands for a minute. Cold liquid in an ear is genuinely unpleasant and your cat will remember.

3. Wrap or support your cat. A towel burrito with the head out works well. If your cat is struggling hard, stop - forcing it risks a bite and makes the next attempt harder.

4. Lift the ear flap gently and look at what you can see. Do not go hunting for anything deeper.

5. Squeeze the cleaner into the canal opening as directed on the label, usually a few drops. Gently massage the base of the ear for 10 to 20 seconds - you will hear a soft squelching.

6. Let your cat shake. This is part of the process, not a failure. Step back and let it happen.

7. Wipe what comes up with a soft cotton pad or gauze, working only on the parts you can see. One clean pad per wipe, and stop when the pad comes away mostly clean.

8. Reward immediately with a treat or a play session. Short and calm beats thorough and stressful.

Never use cotton buds down the canal. You will push debris deeper and you risk perforating the eardrum. A visible fold on the outside only.

What a healthy ear should look like

  • Pale pink to light grey skin, with no angry redness.
  • A small amount of pale yellow-brown wax, soft, not crumbly.
  • No smell beyond a faint, neutral cat smell.
  • No tenderness when you touch the base.

Signs something is actually wrong

  • Dark brown, crumbly debris that looks like coffee grounds. Classic for ear mites - see our breakdown of cat ear mites.
  • A bad yeasty or foul smell. Usually infection, sometimes an underlying allergy.
  • Head shaking, scratching at one ear, or rubbing the ear along the floor.
  • A head tilt, loss of balance or circling. This is not a cleaning problem - it is urgent.
  • A swollen, squishy ear flap. That is an aural haematoma and it needs surgical attention.
  • Discharge, bleeding or a wound. Do not clean over it; have it looked at.

One key point: if an ear hurts, cleaning it hurts more. Cats have a strong bite reflex when their ears are painful. If your cat flinches as soon as you touch the base, stop and book an appointment.

When to see a vet

See your vet if your cat has ear odour, dark discharge, persistent head shaking or scratching, redness or swelling of the ear flap, a head tilt, or signs of pain when the base of the ear is touched. Same-day care is needed for loss of balance, circling, a head tilt with vomiting, bleeding from the ear, or sudden severe pain. Never put cleaner or medicated drops into an ear that might be infected or have a damaged eardrum without veterinary advice, so if an ear hurts or smells, get it examined before you clean it. This article is general education and not veterinary advice - cleaning cannot diagnose or cure an infection, and a cat with a sore ear needs an examination, not a better technique.

Making it easier next time

Clip nails a day before to reduce the damage if things go sideways. Keep sessions under two minutes and always finish with something good, even if you only managed one ear. For the broader routine, pair ear checks with the rest of the grooming and paws routine, and pick up cat-safe ear cleaner, cotton pads and a soft towel from the shop.

The bottom line

Look at your cat's ears monthly and clean only when you see debris, using a cat-safe ear cleaner and a cotton pad on the visible flap - no buds, no peroxide, no water. If there is smell, dark crumbly wax, shaking, scratching or tenderness, book a vet visit instead of cleaning harder. For most cats, the correct amount of ear cleaning is far less than owners expect.

FAQs

How often should I clean my cat's ears?

For a healthy cat that grooms itself, once a month is plenty - and some need far less. Clean more often only if your vet has prescribed it for a condition such as an allergy or a mite infestation. Wiping daily when there is no debris strips the natural oils and irritates the skin.

Can I use cotton buds or hydrogen peroxide?

Neither. Cotton buds push debris deeper and risk perforating the eardrum, and hydrogen peroxide, alcohol and vinegar can damage the delicate lining of the canal. Use a veterinary cat ear cleaner and a soft cotton pad, and only on the parts of the ear you can actually see.

My cat's ears have dark crumbly dirt - what is it?

Dark brown debris that looks like coffee grounds is the classic appearance of ear mites, especially in a cat that shakes its head and scratches. It can also be a yeast infection or a bacterial one, and the treatments differ. Have your vet confirm what it is rather than treating blindly.

Should I clean my cat's ears if they smell bad?

A bad smell almost always means infection or an underlying allergy, and cleaning alone will not fix it. Have the ear examined first. A vet can check whether the eardrum is intact, which decides which products are safe, and prescribe treatment that addresses the cause.
