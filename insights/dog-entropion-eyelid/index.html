Entropion in Dogs: When Rolled Eyelids Need Surgery

Eye Care & Tear Stains · 2026-11-14

Entropion is an eyelid margin that rolls inward, so skin and hair press against the cornea every time your dog blinks. Eye drops will not straighten it, and in most adult dogs the fix is surgery — the only question is how soon. A dog that squints, tears up and rubs its face along the carpet needs an eye exam now, because a cornea can go from irritated to ulcerated inside a week.

What entropion looks like in real life

Owners almost never arrive with the word "entropion". They describe symptoms:

  • Squinting, or holding one eye half shut, worse in bright sun
  • A wet tear track down the face, often with rusty brown staining
  • Discharge that keeps returning no matter how often you wipe it
  • Pawing at the face, or dragging the muzzle across the sofa and rug
  • Pulling away when the head is touched near the eye
  • In heavily coated breeds, an eye that looks buried behind a fold of skin

Turn your dog's head toward a window and look at the lower lid from the side. A normal lid edge sits flat against the eyeball with only a sliver of pink lining showing. With entropion the edge curls in, and that lining disappears from view entirely.

Why it happens: anatomy, not infection

Entropion is structural. The lid is too long for the eye, the cartilage that supports it has folded, or loose skin and fat push the margin inward. That is why it clusters around certain head shapes: Chow Chows, Shar Peis, English Bulldogs, Pugs, Pekingese, Saint Bernards, Rottweilers and Great Danes top most clinic lists.

Two patterns matter, and they are treated very differently:

  • Primary entropion — inherited lid shape, shows up young, usually both eyes.
  • Spastic entropion — the lid turns in because the eye already hurts and the dog is squinting hard. Fix the original pain (an ulcer, a foreign body, conjunctivitis) and the lid may sit normally again.

Operating on a spastic lid that is merely reacting to pain is a real surgical error. That is why a vet will always stain the cornea and measure tear production before booking anything.

The damage that happens quietly

Every blink drags hair across the eye surface. At first the eye just looks wet and red. Then come the changes you cannot see: superficial scratches that light up green with dye, pigment creeping across the cornea, and in long-standing cases a scar that blocks light. A dog can lose usable vision in an eye that never looked badly swollen, which is why "it's been like that a year and he seems fine" is not a safe place to stand.

Corneal ulcers are the acute risk, and they can deepen fast. Sudden holding-shut, a cloudy spot, or a change in the colour of the eye is urgent, not "watch it over the weekend".

How a vet works it out

There is no lab test. Diagnosis is by examination plus the tests that rule out the look-alikes:

1. Fluorescein stain to check for an ulcer — always first, because it changes which drops are safe.

2. Schirmer tear test to measure tear production. Dry eye causes the same squinting and discharge and needs lifelong lubricants, not surgery.

3. Lid and lash exam under a bright light, sometimes after numbing drops, to judge how much of the margin is turned in.

4. Eye pressure check if glaucoma is on the list.

5. Both eyes, because entropion is usually bilateral even when one side looks worse.

Puppies and adults get different fixes

In a young puppy whose skull is still growing, a vet may place temporary tacking sutures that roll the lid out and buy time. Some puppies outgrow the problem enough to need nothing further. It is a holding measure, not a cure, and it only works while the eye surface stays healthy.

Adults with structural entropion need surgery — a wedge of skin is removed and the margin is rolled back out, classically the Hotz-Celsus procedure. Done well, relief is immediate; most dogs go home the same day with pain relief and a cone. If the cornea is already ulcerated, the vet may treat the surface first and operate once it has stabilised.

What helps at home while you wait

Home care cannot reverse anatomy, but it protects the eye and keeps the dog comfortable:

  • Clean the eye with a soft wipe and plain saline, wiping downward and away from the eye.
  • Use only drops your vet prescribed. Never put a steroid drop in an eye that has not been stained — steroids can turn a small ulcer into a melting one.
  • Ask about a lubricant gel if the eye looks dry between blinks.
  • Use a cone if your dog paws at the face. Self-trauma is the most common complication.
  • Trim long hair around the eye, or have a groomer do it, so hair is not dragged in with every blink.
  • Skip dusty car rides, open windows at speed and freshly mown grass for a few days.

For everyday cleaning technique and how to tell normal from abnormal discharge, see our eye care guide and the breakdown of eye discharge colors. A cone, a bottle of sterile saline and soft eye wipes are the three things worth keeping in the cupboard — ours sit in the shop.

When to see a vet

Same-day or emergency care if the eye is suddenly held shut, the surface looks cloudy or blue-grey, there is a visible dip or dark spot, the eye is bulging, or your dog yelps when the face is touched. Book a routine appointment within a few days for a chronically wet eye, a persistent squint, or a lid you can see rolling in — even if your dog seems unbothered. Dogs under-report eye pain, and corneal damage does not heal on its own.

The bottom line

Get a vet exam with a fluorescein stain before you try any drops, and if the lid margin is genuinely rolled in, plan for surgery rather than a lifetime of wiping — early correction protects vision that pigment and scar tissue will not give back. Until the appointment, keep the eye clean with saline, put a cone on any dog that paws at its face, and use only drops your vet has prescribed.

FAQs

Can entropion in dogs heal on its own?

Only in some puppies whose skull is still growing, and usually with temporary tacking sutures placed by a vet. In adult dogs the lid shape is structural, so the margin stays turned in and the cornea keeps getting abraded. Waiting does not improve the anatomy, it just adds pigment and scar tissue.

Will my dog need surgery on both eyes?

Often yes. Entropion is usually bilateral even when one side is obviously worse, and many vets correct both lids during a single anaesthetic so the dog does not face two recoveries. The decision is made at the exam, based on how each margin sits.

How long is recovery after entropion surgery?

Expect a cone for 10 to 14 days, pain relief for the first few days, and suture removal around day 10 to 14. Most dogs are visibly more comfortable within 24 to 48 hours because the rubbing has stopped. Strenuous activity and off-lead running are usually restricted until the sutures come out.

Is entropion the same as cherry eye?

No. Entropion is the eyelid rolling inward; cherry eye is the tear gland of the third eyelid prolapsing outward as a red fleshy lump. Both are common in similar breeds and both can need surgery, but they are different problems with different operations.
