Dog Red Eye: 7 Causes and When It's a Real Emergency

Eye Care & Tear Stains · 2026-10-01

Bottom line: A dog's red eye ranges from mild irritation to a sight-threatening emergency. Rinse only surface grit, never use human eye drops, and treat squinting, cloudiness, or bulging as urgent — the eye doesn't forgive waiting.

A red eye in your dog is one of those symptoms that ranges from annoying to call-the-clinic-now. The color tells you something's inflamed — but whether it's a scratched cornea or just allergies changes everything about what you should do next. When in doubt, the eye is one place you don't wait and see.

First, what red eye can mean

Redness can sit in different spots, and the location is your first clue:

  • Around the white — conjunctivitis or irritation.
  • On the colored part (the iris) — uveitis, which is serious.
  • A red lump at the corner — possible cherry eye.
  • The whole eye looks bloodshot and bulging — glaucoma or trauma, both emergencies.

The 7 most common causes

1. Conjunctivitis (pink eye) — the most common. Eyes look red, weepy, and may have discharge. Often allergies or mild irritation; contagious forms exist between dogs.

2. Corneal ulcer — a scratch on the clear front of the eye. Dogs with this squint, paw at the face, and the eye waters constantly. Ulcers can perforate in days.

3. Cherry eye — a tear-gland prolapse that pops up as a red, cherry-like lump at the inner corner. Not usually painful at first, but it won't go back on its own.

4. Glaucoma — pressure inside the eye spikes; the eye looks red, cloudy, and often bulging, with a dilated pupil. This is an emergency — vision can be lost in hours.

5. Allergies — seasonal pollen or dust makes eyes red and itchy, usually with clear discharge and some face-rubbing.

6. Dry eye (KCS) — the eye doesn't make enough tears, so it stays red, goopy, and sore. Left alone, it scars the cornea.

7. Foreign body — a grass seed or bit of grit trapped under the lid. Sudden violent blinking and pawing are the giveaway.

A gentle flush with a sterile-style eye wash from our eye care guide can remove surface grit — but don't dig, and don't assume it's just allergies if your dog is in obvious pain.

What you can safely do at home

  • Rinse only with a pet eye wash if there's visible debris and your dog is calm.
  • Avoid human eye drops — many contain ingredients unsafe for dogs, and some with steroids make ulcers far worse.
  • Protect from rubbing — an e-collar prevents your dog from scratching the eye raw while you decide on the next step.
When to see a vet — immediately: Squinting, pawing, a cloudy or bulging eye, a fixed dilated pupil, yellow or green discharge, sudden vision loss, or any eye injury from a fight or object. These are genuine emergencies where hours cost sight. Even mild red eye that hasn't improved in 24 hours, or that returns after a day, deserves a vet look. How vets actually diagnose it

Expect a stain test (fluorescein drops that light up ulcers), a pressure check for glaucoma, and a close look under magnification. None are pleasant for a worried owner, but they take minutes and tell the vet exactly what they're treating. The right diagnosis matters: the drops that fix allergies can blind a dog with an ulcer.

Can you prevent red eye?

Partly. You can't stop every scratch, but you can lower the odds:

  • Trim face fur on long-haired breeds so hair doesn't poke the eye.
  • Rinse after dusty walks with a pet eye wash to clear pollen and grit.
  • Keep chemicals away — don't let your dog stick its head into cleaning sprays or pool chlorine.
  • Watch flat-faced breeds (pugs, bulldogs) closely — their shallow eye sockets make injury and proptosis more likely. The right eye wipes and drops are in our shop.

The bottom line

A dog's red eye ranges from mild irritation to a sight-threatening emergency. Rinse only surface grit, never use human eye drops, and treat squinting, cloudiness, or bulging as urgent — the eye doesn't forgive waiting.

Sources: VCA Animal Hospitals — Eye Conditions in Dogs; Cornell University College of Veterinary Medicine — Ophthalmology.

FAQs

How can I tell if my dog's red eye is an emergency?

Squinting, pawing, a cloudy or bulging eye, green or yellow discharge, a fixed dilated pupil, or sudden vision loss mean go now. Plain redness with clear discharge and no pain is more likely mild irritation — but if it lasts over 24 hours, see a vet.

Can I use human eye drops on my dog?

No. Many human drops, especially steroid ones, are unsafe for dogs and can worsen a corneal ulcer or raise eye pressure. Use only vet-prescribed or pet-specific eye products.

Will a dog's red eye heal on its own?

Mild allergic or irritant redness sometimes settles once the trigger is removed, but ulcers, glaucoma, and foreign bodies will not and can cost the eye. A quick vet stain test rules out the dangerous causes.

What does cherry eye look like?

A smooth red, cherry-shaped lump at the inner corner of the eye. It's a prolapsed gland, not usually painful at first, but it needs veterinary repair to avoid dry eye later.
