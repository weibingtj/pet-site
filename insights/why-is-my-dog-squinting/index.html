Why Is My Dog Squinting? 7 Common Causes Explained

Eye Care & Tear Stains · 2026-10-26

Bottom line: Squinting means a painful eye, so put a cone on, skip the home remedies and book a vet exam the same day - the fluorescein stain takes minutes and is the difference between drops that fix a scratch and blindness that started as one.

A squinting dog is a dog in eye pain, and the squint itself is the tell - most often caused by a corneal scratch or ulcer, something trapped under the eyelid, inflammation inside the eye, or glaucoma. Dogs rarely squint for style; a held-shut eye nearly always hurts, and a painful eye is a same-day or next-day vet visit, not a watch-and-wait project.

The reason for the urgency is simple: the commonest causes of squinting - corneal ulcers - can progress from a small scratch to a melting, rupturing ulcer within 24 to 48 hours, and glaucoma can cause permanent blindness in a matter of days. Fast action preserves vision; delays can cost it.

The seven usual suspects

1. Corneal ulcer (scratched eye surface) - the number one cause. A twig, grass seed, claw or a rumble with the cat scrapes the clear front of the eye. You may see watering, redness, rubbing at the face and light sensitivity alongside the squint.

2. Foreign body - a grass seed behind the third eyelid, sand, or a stray eyelash. Squinting starts suddenly, often after a walk through long grass or a windy beach day.

3. Conjunctivitis - inflammation of the pink tissue lining the lids. The eye looks red and stringy-discharged, and can feel gritty enough to make your dog keep it half shut. Allergies and infections both do this.

4. Glaucoma - pressure building inside the eye. The eye may look slightly enlarged or cloudy, the pupil wide, and the pain is severe - some dogs become quiet and off food rather than pawing at the face. This is a true emergency.

5. Uveitis (inflammation inside the eye) - often linked to infection, immune disease or injury. Light hurts, so the dog squints in bright rooms even more.

6. Eyelid problems - entropion (lids rolling inward so lashes scrape the eye) is common in breeds like shar-peis, bulldogs and retrievers. The squint is chronic and worsens after grooming or irritation flares.

7. Dry eye (keratoconjunctivitis sicca) - too little tear film leaves the surface burning. Look for thick, sticky discharge and a dull-looking eye; squinting tends to build over weeks rather than start suddenly.

Flat-faced breeds - pugs, shih tzus, Boston terriers - squint more often than most because their prominent eyes get injured more easily, and they are also the breeds where ulcers turn nasty fastest.

What you can safely check at home

  • Compare the two eyes in good light: is one cloudier, redder, smaller or more bulging than the other?
  • Look for a visible foreign body on the eye surface or under the lids. If you see one, do not dig for it - a vet can flush it properly. If it is a loose hair on the surface, flushing with sterile saline is fine.
  • Note the timeline - sudden onset points to injury or a foreign body; a slow build-up suggests dry eye, entropion or chronic inflammation.
  • Prevent rubbing. An Elizabethan collar feels dramatic and works. Every paw swipe adds damage to whatever is already wrong.

Do not use leftover eye drops, human drops, or any salt-water mixes made in the kitchen - some human preparations and home remedies actively worsen ulcers.

Why the vet visit matters

The single most useful test is fluorescein staining: a harmless orange dye that turns green wherever the cornea is scraped, making an invisible ulcer obvious in seconds. Pressure testing rules glaucoma in or out. From there, treatment is targeted - antibiotic drops for ulcers, anti-inflammatory drops for uveitis, pressure-lowering medication or surgery for glaucoma, surgery to correct rolling lids.

An ulcer that is deep, cloudy, cratered, or that has a bulging centre is an emergency referral situation, not a routine one. When to see a vet

See a vet the same day if the squint started suddenly, the eye is cloudy, blue or bulging, there is heavy discharge, your dog is pawing at the face, or a puppy or flat-faced breed is involved. Treat any squint lasting more than a few hours as painful until a vet has stained and examined the eye - and go immediately for a suspected foreign body or glaucoma signs.

The bottom line

Squinting means a painful eye, so put a cone on, skip the home remedies and book a vet exam the same day - the fluorescein stain takes minutes and is the difference between drops that fix a scratch and blindness that started as one.

FAQs

Can a dog's eye ulcer heal on its own?

Minor surface scrapes sometimes do, but you cannot tell a simple abrasion from a deepening ulcer without a fluorescein dye stain, and ulcers can worsen dramatically within 24 hours. Any squinting, painful eye deserves a vet exam and staining rather than a wait-and-see approach.

Why does my dog squint in bright light but not indoors?

Light sensitivity points to inflammation on or inside the eye - corneal ulcers and uveitis both make bright light painful. Treat it as a reason to book an exam: the eye is telling you that light itself hurts.

My dog squints one eye after walks in long grass - is it allergies?

Pollen can irritate eyes, but a repeat pattern of one-eye squinting after walks is a classic grass seed or scratch history. A seed trapped behind the third eyelid keeps re-injuring the surface until a vet finds and removes it, so it is worth an examination.

Is squinting ever just sleepiness?

Yes - dozing dogs and relaxed, content dogs half-close their eyes normally. A painful squint is different: it comes with watering, redness, rubbing or light avoidance, and it persists when the dog is awake and engaged.
