Dog Ear Hematoma: Why the Flap Swells and What Fixes It

Grooming, Paws & Ears · 2026-11-04

Then keep treating the ear for weeks after it looks normal, because stopping early is what brings the next hematoma.

A dog ear hematoma is a pocket of blood between the ear cartilage and the skin, and it does not quietly resolve on its own. The swelling is a symptom: something made your dog shake its head hard enough to burst a small vessel, so the ear and the shaking both need treating.

What an aural hematoma actually is

The ear flap is a sandwich: skin on the outside, a thin plate of cartilage in the middle, and loose tissue in between where the blood vessels run. When one of those vessels tears, blood pumps into that potential space and separates the layers. What you see is a soft, warm, doughy swelling on the inside of the flap, sometimes the whole ear, sometimes just the tip.

It is not a tumour, which is the first thing most owners fear when the ear doubles in size overnight. It is also not a bruise that will fade in a week. Blood in that space clots, organises, and then scars, and scarring is what leaves the ear thick and folded.

Why it happens: the itch-shake loop

Almost every hematoma starts with head shaking or ear scratching. The force is absurd: a dog can generate many times the force of gravity at the ear tip, and a small vessel gives way. So the useful question is not what the swelling is, but why the shaking started.

  • Ear infection (otitis externa) is the most common trigger, especially in floppy-eared dogs
  • Ear mites, more common in puppies but possible at any age
  • Allergies, food or environmental, which drive itch without much visible debris
  • A grass awn or foreign body lodged down the canal
  • Recurrent skin disease or thyroid problems in dogs who get these more than once

If your dog was pawing at the ear, rubbing it on furniture, or holding the head tilted for a few days before the swelling appeared, that history matters more than the size of the flap. The pattern to look for is covered in dog ear infection signs.

Breeds with pendulous ears such as Labradors, Golden Retrievers, Cocker Spaniels and Basset Hounds are over-represented, and so are dogs with tight, narrow canals where debris cannot drain.

Why needle drainage alone often fails

Most vets will drain a fresh, small hematoma to relieve pressure, and for a day it looks fixed because the ear goes flat. Then it refills within 24 to 72 hours. You removed the blood, not the leaking vessel, and you left a cavity that fills right back up.

Repeated drainage also introduces bacteria and increases the chance of a permanently thickened, crumpled ear once it finally scars down.

The three real options

1. Conservative management. Some small hematomas in dogs that stop shaking will resolve with pain relief, cold compresses in the first 48 hours, and treatment of the underlying ear disease. Reasonable for a small, flat swelling in a calm older dog. Slow, and the ear usually heals a little thicker.

2. Drainage plus a temporary drain. Keeps the pocket open so it can keep draining while it heals. Cheaper than surgery, decent cosmetic result, needs a cone and a recheck in a few days.

3. Surgical repair. The standard for large or recurrent hematomas. The vet opens the flap, removes the clot, and stitches cartilage to skin in a pattern that closes the dead space. Best cosmetic outcome and the lowest refill rate, at the cost of anaesthesia and a longer healing window.

There is no single correct choice. Size, how long it has been there, the dog's age and anaesthetic risk, and whether the ear disease can be controlled all change the answer, so the conversation with your vet should be about trade-offs rather than one "best" procedure.

Recovery, honestly

Expect two to four weeks. The ear will look worse before it looks better: swelling, bruising and crusting along the incision are normal in the first ten days, and a small amount of fresh blood at the drain site is not an emergency. The cone stays on until your vet says otherwise, usually 10 to 14 days. Taking it off early because the dog seems fine is the single most common reason a repair fails.

No off-lead running, no head-shaking games, no wrestling, and no swimming for the first two weeks. Keep the ear dry and check it daily for a smell or spreading redness.

Breaking the loop so it does not come back

A meaningful share of hematomas recur when the underlying ear disease is left alone, so the follow-up plan matters as much as the procedure:

  • Get the canal examined, not just the flap
  • Ask for cytology so treatment matches bacteria, yeast, or neither
  • Finish the full course of ear medication even after the head shaking stops
  • Keep nails short and check ears weekly in allergy-prone dogs
  • Dry ears properly after swimming or bathing, as covered in the grooming, paws and ears guide

When to see a vet

Book an appointment within 24 hours for any new ear swelling, and the same day if the ear is hot, very painful to touch, bleeding, or if your dog is tilting its head and losing balance. Rapid swelling, discharge with a bad smell, or a dog who will not let anyone near the head all need hands-on assessment. This article cannot replace that exam, and pain is not something a dog should simply tolerate until Monday.

The bottom line

Get any new ear swelling examined within a day, have the canal treated for the infection or allergy behind it, and choose drainage or surgery based on size and your dog's anaesthetic risk. Then keep treating the ear for weeks after it looks normal, because stopping early is what brings the next hematoma.

Sources: AAHA 2015 Otitis Externa Guidelines for Dogs and Cats; VCA Animal Hospitals: Ear Hematoma in Dogs; Merck Veterinary Manual: Diseases of the Auricle and Ear Canal.

FAQs

Can a dog ear hematoma heal without treatment?

A small one sometimes does, but it usually heals thicker and can leave the ear permanently folded. Because the cause is still active, the safer plan is a vet exam, drainage or surgery, and treatment for the underlying ear disease.

Is an ear hematoma painful for my dog?

Yes. The flap is densely supplied with nerves and the pressure of the blood pocket is uncomfortable. Pain relief is part of correct treatment, so ask for it rather than assuming your dog is coping.

How long does ear hematoma surgery take to heal?

Most ears are comfortable within a week and healed by two to four weeks. The cone normally stays on for 10 to 14 days, and the ear may stay slightly thicker than the other one even after full healing.

Can I drain my dog's ear at home?

No. Home drainage risks infection, does not remove the leaking vessel, and makes later repair harder. Put a cone on to stop the shaking and get the ear assessed.
