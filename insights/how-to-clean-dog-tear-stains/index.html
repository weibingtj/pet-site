How to Clean Dog Tear Stains: A Safe Daily Routine

Eye Care & Tear Stains · 2026-10-03

Bottom line: Tear stains are usually cosmetic, but the moisture under the eyes deserves daily care. A gentle flush-dissolve-wipe routine keeps the zone dry and healthy, while squinting or one-eye-only tearing means a vet visit.

Tear stains are the reddish-brown marks under a dog's eyes, caused when tears spill onto the fur and the pigments in those tears oxidize in sunlight. They're usually cosmetic — but the moisture underneath is worth managing, and some staining actually points to a real eye problem.

Why tears stain the fur

Tears are meant to drain through small ducts into the nose. When they overflow (epiphora), they run down the face instead. Light-colored breeds show the staining worst because the pigments, called porphyrins, turn rusty as they oxidize. Contributing factors include flat-faced anatomy, shallow ducts, allergies, dust, and even the type of water bowl.

The gentle daily routine

A light, consistent touch does more than aggressive scrubbing, which only irritates skin and can worsen tearing. Four steps:

1. Flush — a sterile-style pet eye wash clears dust and loose discharge without touching the eye. Use a soft cloth or cotton pad, never a pointed swab near the eyeball.

2. Dissolve — a stain-removing liquid applied to the fur (never in the eye) loosens built-up pigment. Let it sit per the product directions.

3. Wipe — hypoallergenic wipes dry the area. Moisture is what lets yeast and bacteria grow, so keeping it dry is the real secret.

4. Maintain — once stains fade, a 2–3× weekly wipe-down holds the line.

Products that actually help

  • Eye wash / rinse — for the skin around the eye and surface grit.
  • Stain-removal solution — formulated for the fur, not the eye itself.
  • Hypoallergenic wipes — for daily drying and upkeep.
  • Stainless steel bowls — some dogs react to plastic; switching bowls reduces chin and eye-area irritation for a few pets.

Mistakes that make staining worse

Rubbing hard, using human makeup remover, or putting any product in the eye itself can inflame the skin and increase tearing. Treat the zone gently and keep it dry — that combination outperforms any stronger product.

When to see a vet

If you notice squinting, redness of the eye itself, pawing at the face, sudden heavy discharge, or tearing in only one eye, book a vet exam. Those signs point to blocked ducts, ingrown lashes, ulcers, or infection that no cosmetic wipe can fix. This guide covers everyday maintenance only — it is not a substitute for veterinary care.

A quick vet evaluation rules out the medical causes and tells you whether you're dealing with cosmetics or a condition that needs treatment.

FAQs

Can I use human makeup remover on my dog's tear stains?

No. Human products aren't formulated for the thin skin around a dog's eyes and can sting or inflame it, which increases tearing. Use only pet-specific eye wipes and rinses.

How long until dog tear stains fade?

Because stained fur has to grow out, expect 3–8 weeks of consistent daily care. Keeping the area dry speeds it up far more than scrubbing harder.

Are some breeds more prone to tear stains?

Yes. Maltese, Shih Tzus, Poodles, Bichons, and Persians stain more due to facial anatomy and shallow tear drainage, so maintenance is usually lifelong.

Should I trim the fur under my dog's eyes?

Lightly trimming the hair under the eyes reduces the surface that stays damp and catches pigment, but use round-tip scissors and go slowly — or let a groomer do it to avoid accidents near the eye.
