Dog Facial Fold Cleaning: A Safe Routine for Wrinkly Breeds

Grooming, Paws & Ears · 2026-11-09

Bottom line: Clean the folds as often as they stay dirty, dry them completely every single time, and keep the hair inside them short. Drying matters more than the product, and any fold that is red, smelly or painful needs a vet before you clean it again.

Facial fold problems in Pugs, French Bulldogs, English Bulldogs and Pekingese are mostly a moisture problem, not a dirt problem. Clean the fold, then dry it completely, and keep the hair inside it short. Skip the drying step and no wipe on the market will hold an infection off.

Why folds get sore in the first place

A fold is skin pressed against skin, and that creates three conditions bacteria and yeast like. Friction rubs the surface layer thin. Trapped warmth and moisture soften the skin further. Air cannot circulate, so the area stays humid for hours after a drink, a nap or a walk in the rain.

Malassezia yeast and Staphylococcus bacteria then overgrow. The result is called skin fold dermatitis, or intertrigo, and it looks like this:

  • Redness and a moist, sometimes eroded line deep in the crease
  • Brown greasy discharge that leaves a smear on a tissue
  • A sour, musty or yeasty smell that returns within a day of cleaning
  • Hair loss along the fold, and the dog rubbing its face on carpet or furniture
  • Head-shy behaviour, pulling away when the face is touched

If you recognise more than two of those, stop the cosmetic routine and get the fold looked at.

Which folds to check, and when

Most owners only clean the nose fold. Brachycephalic dogs usually have several, and the ones that get missed are the ones that flare:

1. The nose fold, the horizontal crease above the nose, worst in Pugs and Pekingese

2. Lip folds, especially in spaniels and Bulldogs, where saliva and food collect

3. The tail fold, tight against the body in English Bulldogs and Pugs

4. Vulvar or preputial folds, in obese dogs and recessed anatomy

5. Neck and body rolls, in Shar-Pei and overweight dogs

Check all of them once a week even if you only clean the face daily. A tail fold abscess can brew for days before the dog shows obvious pain.

The daily routine that works

1. Wet a pad, do not scrub. Use vet-approved fold wipes or plain sterile saline on gauze. Damp is enough; soaking the fold adds the very moisture you are fighting.

2. Wipe along the crease, one direction. Work from the clean end of the fold outward, using a fresh pad for each fold. Never wipe the eye area with the same pad used on a smelly lip fold.

3. Dry it properly, every time. Fold dry gauze or a soft tissue into the crease and press gently until it comes away dry. This is the step that prevents most infections, and it is the step almost everyone skips.

4. Keep the hair short. Ask your groomer to clip the fold line, or use a small clipper carefully at home. Long hair wicks moisture in and holds debris against the skin.

5. Add a barrier only if your vet agrees. A thin vet-recommended barrier ointment can help a dog that stays damp, but it must go on clean, dry skin. Applying it over discharge seals the infection in.

Frequency should follow the dog, not a schedule you read online. A shallow, well-ventilated fold may need cleaning two or three times a week. A deep moist fold, a dog with a history of infections, or a humid summer week calls for daily work. During an active flare your vet will usually prescribe a medicated wipe such as chlorhexidine with miconazole once or twice daily for one to two weeks, then step down to maintenance.

What not to put in a fold

  • Alcohol or hydrogen peroxide: painful, dries and damages the skin barrier, and makes the next infection easier
  • Scented baby wipes: fragrance and preservatives irritate already inflamed skin
  • Baby powder, talc or cornstarch: they cake into paste in a damp crease and feed yeast, which is the opposite of what you want
  • Cotton buds pushed deep into the nose crease: easy to abrade the skin and impossible to see what you are doing
  • Human antiseptic or steroid creams: a dog's fold skin is thin and absorbs well, and steroid creams can thin it further and mask an infection that is still active
  • Daily medicated shampoo on intact skin: over-treatment causes irritation; reserve medicated products for the cadence your vet sets

One more rule that saves a lot of trouble: a dog with red, swollen, painful or bleeding folds is not a grooming case. Cleaning an infected fold without treatment just spreads discharge around, and a dog that is head-shy may be telling you the fold hurts, not that it dislikes wipes.

When to see a vet

Book an appointment if a fold is red, swollen, hot, bleeding, has a foul smell after cleaning, or if the dog yelps when the area is touched. A fold abscess, a deep intertrigo or an ear infection tracking down from the canal all need treatment, not more wiping. Dogs that are unwell, off food or running a temperature with a sore fold should be seen the same day. Never lance or squeeze a swollen fold at home.

The bottom line

Clean the folds as often as they stay dirty, dry them completely every single time, and keep the hair inside them short. Drying matters more than the product, and any fold that is red, smelly or painful needs a vet before you clean it again.

FAQs

How often should I clean my Pug's facial folds?

Follow the fold, not a fixed calendar. A shallow, airy fold often needs cleaning two or three times a week, while a deep moist crease, a humid week or a dog with a history of infections needs it daily. Check all the folds weekly even if you clean only the face each day.

Can I use baby wipes on dog wrinkles?

Better not. Most baby wipes contain fragrance and preservatives that irritate inflamed skin, and they leave the fold damp. Sterile saline on gauze or a vet-approved fold wipe, followed by thorough drying, is the safer routine.

Why does my dog's fold smell yeasty again the next day?

Usually because the fold stayed damp after cleaning, or because an active Malassezia infection was never treated. Yeast needs moisture to rebound. Dry the crease fully, keep the hair clipped, and if the smell returns within a day, ask your vet about a medicated wipe course.

Is it normal for a dog to hate having its folds cleaned?

Some resistance is normal, especially at first. Sudden head-shy behaviour in a dog that used to tolerate it is not, and often means the fold is painful. Go slowly, reward with food, and have a vet check the area if the change is new.
