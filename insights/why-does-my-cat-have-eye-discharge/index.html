Why Does My Cat Have Eye Discharge? Causes & Care

Eye Care & Tear Stains · 2026-09-27

Most cat eye discharge is minor, but the color and consistency tell you a lot. Clear, occasional crust is usually normal; thick, colored, or one-sided discharge often signals something that needs a vet.

What 'normal' looks like

Cats produce a small amount of clear tears that evaporate or form a tiny dry crust in the corner of the eye, especially after sleep. A little brown or black debris at the inner corner — more visible on light-coated cats — is generally harmless and just needs a quick wipe. The problem is when discharge changes in amount, color, or which eye it affects.

Common causes of abnormal discharge

  • Upper respiratory infection ('cat cold'). The single most common cause of runny, watery, or goopy eyes in cats, especially kittens and multi-cat homes. Often comes with sneezing and nasal congestion.
  • Conjunctivitis. Inflammation of the pink tissue around the eye, causing redness, swelling, and discharge. It can be viral, bacterial, or allergy-related.
  • Blocked tear ducts. When ducts can't drain tears, they spill over the face as clear or stained runoff.
  • Corneal scratch or ulcer. A painful defect on the eye's surface, usually from rough play or a foreign body, causing squinting and watery eyes.
  • Breed anatomy. Flat-faced breeds like Persians have shallow eye sockets and often tear constantly — mostly cosmetic, but the wet fur can irritate the skin.

Safe at-home cleaning

For mild, clear crust, you can gently clean the area:

1. Use a warm, damp cotton ball — one per eye, never shared, to avoid spreading infection.

2. Wipe from the inner corner outward, using a fresh side as needed. Don't press on the eyeball.

3. Dry the area afterward; trapped moisture irritates skin and deepens staining.

When to see a vet

When to see a vet: call your veterinarian if the discharge is yellow or green, comes from only one eye, is paired with squinting, redness, or pawing at the face, or if your cat seems off — not eating, hiding, or sneezing heavily. A sudden onset in one eye, any cloudiness, or a visible third eyelid can mean an ulcer or foreign body that needs prompt treatment. Kittens with eye discharge should be seen quickly, as infections spread fast and can threaten sight.

Don't Guess at Drops

It's tempting to borrow dog or human eye drops, but the wrong product can worsen a corneal ulcer or mask a serious infection. Use only a cleanser meant for the skin around the eye, and leave medicated drops to your vet's prescription. This is general guidance, not a diagnosis — when in doubt, a short exam beats a week of guessing.

The bottom line

A little clear crust is part of owning a cat. Anything thick, colored, one-sided, or accompanied by squinting or sickness is a vet conversation, not a wipe-and-wait situation. Catching eye problems early protects both comfort and vision.

FAQs

Is clear eye discharge in my cat normal?

A small amount of clear tear or dry crust, especially after sleep, is usually normal. Worry when it becomes constant, colored, one-sided, or comes with redness, squinting, or sneezing — those point to infection or injury.

Can I use the same wipe for both of my cat's eyes?

No. If one eye is infected, sharing a cotton ball or cloth spreads it to the healthy eye. Always use a fresh, clean cotton ball per eye and wipe from the inner corner outward.

Why is only one of my cat's eyes watering?

One-sided discharge often means a local problem — a blocked duct, scratch, ulcer, or a foreign body in that eye — rather than a systemic cold. Because it can signal something painful, have a vet check a single watering eye.

Do flat-faced cats like Persians always have eye problems?

Not disease, but their anatomy causes chronic tearing that stains the fur and can irritate skin if not kept dry. Daily gentle wiping keeps the area healthy, though persistent redness or thick discharge still warrants a vet look.
