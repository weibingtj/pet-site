Broken Tooth in Dogs: What to Do and When It's Urgent

A broken dog tooth is never just cosmetic: once the pulp is exposed, bacteria move straight into the root, and the tooth usually dies - so it needs a veterinary dental exam within 24 to 48 hours, even if your dog is eating normally. Dogs hide mouth pain exceptionally well, and a dog that chews through dinner without flinching can still be carrying an infected root canal.

What actually broke

Vets group tooth fractures by how deep they go, and the depth decides the treatment.

  • Enamel chip. A small nick in the outer layer only. Often cosmetic, but still worth a look, because a sharp edge can cut the lip or tongue.
  • Uncomplicated crown fracture. Enamel and dentine are broken but the pulp (the nerve and blood supply in the middle) is not exposed. Dentine is porous and painful when exposed, and bacteria can still track through it.
  • Complicated crown fracture. The pulp is exposed - you may see a pink dot, a red speck or a black hole in the centre of the break. This is the urgent one.
  • Root fracture or a tooth split vertically below the gumline. Not visible from the outside and only found on dental x-rays.

Signs your dog has a broken tooth

1. A visible chip, crack or missing corner, most often on the upper fourth premolar - the big shearing tooth near the back - or on a canine.

2. A pink, red or black spot in the middle of the break, which means the pulp is open.

3. Chewing on one side, dropping food, or taking longer over meals.

4. Pawing at the mouth, rubbing the muzzle along the floor, or chattering the jaw.

5. Drooling, bad breath, or blood-tinged saliva.

6. Reluctance to play tug, pick up a toy or eat hard food where they used to be fine.

7. A swollen or painful area under the eye - that is a sign of a tooth-root abscess and it is urgent.

What causes it

Chewing on hard objects is almost always the answer: deer antlers, bones, hooves, hard nylon chews, rocks, wire, crate bars, and ice cubes. Dogs also fracture teeth on hard plastic frisbees, on furniture, and in a fall or a road accident. If your dog has broken more than one tooth, the chewing habit is worth changing, not just the teeth.

First aid in the next hour

1. Stay calm and keep your hands out of the mouth if your dog is painful. A dog in mouth pain can bite even a familiar owner.

2. If a piece of tooth came out, pick it up and put it in a small container of saline, or milk if you have no saline. Do not scrub it or let it dry out.

3. Do not pull a loose fragment. A wobbly piece may still be attached to the root, and pulling can break the root and damage the bone.

4. Do not give human painkillers. Ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin and paracetamol are toxic to dogs.

5. Offer soft food and cool water for the next meal, and skip hard chews, bones and tug games entirely.

6. Call your vet and book a dental exam. Say clearly that you suspect a fractured tooth with exposed pulp.

What the vet will do

Your vet will examine the whole mouth, not just the broken tooth, because dental disease is usually not a one-tooth problem. Expect dental x-rays under sedation or anaesthesia - you cannot assess a root from the outside. From there the options are narrowed to three: a root canal by a veterinary dentist, which saves the tooth and the pain; extraction if the tooth is too damaged or already dead; or in rare minor enamel chips, smoothing and monitoring. The one thing that is not an option is leaving an exposed pulp alone, because it leads to an abscess, a draining sinus under the eye, bone loss and chronic pain.

Why "wait and see" backfires

An exposed pulp does not heal. It becomes inflamed, then infected, then necrotic. In the early days your dog may eat normally, because dogs are wired to hide weakness. By the time the signs are obvious - swelling, a smell, a dog that stops chewing on that side - the infection has usually reached the root apex and started to erode bone. Early treatment is cheaper, less painful, and far more likely to save the tooth. If you are wondering whether to watch it a few days, use our dog dental disease signs guide to check for the other giveaways, and keep brushing the rest of the mouth gently - our dental care guide explains how to brush without making a broken tooth worse.

Preventing the next one

  • Retire the hard chews. If you cannot bend it, dent it with a fingernail, or it does not give when you press it, it can break a tooth.
  • Choose purpose-made dental chews that flex, and pick the right size for your dog.
  • Brush most days with pet enzyme toothpaste - three times a week at minimum. Our how often to brush dog teeth guide covers the realistic routine.
  • Keep up professional dental cleanings as your vet recommends, so weakened teeth are found early.
  • Fetch smart. Throw a soft rubber ball or a flying disc designed for dogs, not a hard plastic one.

When to see a vet

Treat a broken tooth as urgent if you can see a pink, red or black spot in the middle of the break - that is exposed pulp and it needs a dental exam within 24 to 48 hours. Go to a vet the same day if there is bleeding that will not stop, a swollen face or swelling under the eye, a bad smell from the mouth, drooling with head shaking, or a dog that cannot or will not eat. In a puppy with a fractured baby tooth, call your vet too, because the injury can damage the adult tooth still forming underneath. Any broken tooth with a piece missing and no fragment recovered should also be checked, in case the fragment was swallowed. This article is general education and not veterinary advice.

The bottom line

A broken dog tooth is never just cosmetic - once the pulp is exposed, bacteria travel straight into the root and the tooth almost always dies, so the tooth needs a veterinary dental exam within 24 to 48 hours even if your dog is eating normally and showing no pain. Do not pull the fragment or give human painkillers, and keep the piece in saline or milk if a shard comes loose.

FAQs

Do I need to see a vet for a chipped dog tooth?

Yes, get it looked at. A small enamel chip may only need smoothing and monitoring, but you cannot tell from the outside whether the dentine or the pulp is involved. A quick exam settles it, and if the pulp is exposed the clock is running on saving the tooth.

My dog broke a tooth but seems fine. Can I wait?

Dogs hide dental pain well, so seeming fine is not reassurance. If the pulp is exposed, the tooth will die and can abscess within days to weeks. Aim for a veterinary dental exam within 24 to 48 hours of finding a fracture, especially if you can see a pink, red or black centre.

Will a broken tooth heal on its own?

No. Enamel and dentine do not regrow, and an exposed pulp cannot heal itself. It becomes inflamed, then infected, and usually necrotic, which is why a fractured tooth needs treatment - a root canal to save it or an extraction to remove the source of pain and infection.

Can I give my dog painkillers for a broken tooth?

Not human ones. Ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin and paracetamol are toxic to dogs and can cause stomach ulcers or kidney damage. Call your vet, who can prescribe a dog-safe painkiller and book the dental exam at the same time.
