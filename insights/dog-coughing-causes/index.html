Dog Coughing: 7 Common Causes and When to Call a Vet

General · 2026-10-14

Bottom line: Most mild coughs in a bright, eating dog settle within one to two weeks with rest, a harness instead of a neck collar and clean, smoke-free air - but book a vet visit the same day if the cough is wet, blood-tinged, lasts more than a few days, or comes with laboured breathing, fainting, fever or a dog that will not eat. A sudden, violent cough that started in one moment is an emergency, because it may be something stuck in the airway.

A cough in an otherwise bright, eating dog is usually short-lived and often caused by kennel cough, but a cough that is wet, blood-tinged, comes on with exercise or fainting, lasts more than a few days, or appears with laboured breathing needs a vet. You can narrow the cause down a long way at home by listening to the sound, noting when it happens and watching for the red flags below. This guide is educational only - a cough is a symptom, not a diagnosis, and only your vet can examine the airways, heart and lungs.

What a cough actually is

A cough is a reflex that clears the airway. Something irritates the lining of the throat, windpipe or bronchi, and the body fires out a burst of air to shift it. That means the cause can sit almost anywhere from the larynx down to the lungs - or even outside the airway entirely, because an enlarged heart presses on the windpipe and triggers the same reflex.

That is why "just a cough" is a poor description. Two dogs with identical-sounding coughs can have completely different problems, and the pattern around the cough often matters more than the sound of it.

The 7 common causes

1. Kennel cough (infectious tracheobronchitis). A harsh, honking cough that often ends with a retch or foamy spit. It spreads fast wherever dogs mix - boarding, daycare, training class, the park. Most healthy adult dogs settle with rest and supportive care, but puppies, seniors and unwell dogs can worsen.

2. Collapsing trachea. A goose-honk cough triggered by pulling on the lead, excitement or pressure on the neck. It is most common in small breeds such as Yorkshire Terriers, Pomeranians and Chihuahuas.

3. Heart disease. An enlarged heart or a leaky valve presses on the windpipe. The cough is often worse at night, after lying down, or after gentle exercise, and may come with a lower tolerance for walks.

4. Chronic bronchitis. A long-standing, moist cough in middle-aged and older dogs. Smoke, dust, aerosols, strong cleaners and excess body fat all make it worse.

5. A foreign body or grass seed. A sudden, violent, repeated cough that started in one moment, often after sniffing at grass or eating. This is an emergency: the object will not come out on its own.

6. Irritants and reverse sneezing. Smoke, perfumes and cleaning products can all set off coughing. Reverse sneezing looks alarming but is a short snorting episode rather than a true cough.

7. Pneumonia, heartworm or lung disease. A wet cough with fever, lethargy, poor appetite or fast breathing points to infection or fluid in the lungs and needs prompt treatment.

How to describe the cough to your vet

Vets diagnose faster when the history is precise. Before your appointment, note:

  • The sound - dry and honking, moist and rattling, or a single sharp burst.
  • The trigger - exercise, night-time, pulling on the lead, drinking, excitement or nothing obvious.
  • Duration - days or weeks, and whether it is getting better or worse.
  • What comes up - nothing, foam, white mucus, or anything blood-tinged.
  • Other signs - appetite, energy, breathing rate at rest, fainting, weight change.
  • Exposure - recent boarding, daycare, grooming or contact with other dogs.

A short phone video of the cough is genuinely useful. Many owners capture a classic collapsing-trachea honk that never happens in the consulting room.

What helps at home while you wait

Supportive care is reasonable for a mild, recent cough in a dog that is bright, eating and drinking:

  • Rest. Short, calm lead walks only. Running and excitement worsen most airway problems.
  • A harness instead of a collar. Neck pressure directly worsens collapsing trachea and tracheal irritation. Browse harnesses and airway-friendly walking gear.
  • Clean, humid air. Avoid smoke, aerosols, scented candles and strong cleaners, and use a humidifier in dry rooms.
  • Weight control. Extra body fat leaves the lungs and airway less room to work with.
  • Never give human cough medicine. Many contain ingredients toxic to dogs, including paracetamol and some decongestants.

What your vet will check

A diagnosis usually starts with a full physical examination, then narrows with:

  • Listening to the chest for murmurs, crackles or wheeze.
  • Chest X-rays to look at heart size, the windpipe and lung patterns.
  • Airway sampling - a wash or swab when infection is suspected.
  • Blood and faecal tests, including heartworm screening where relevant.

If your dog is also stiff, reluctant to move or sleeping badly, see our guide to how to tell if your dog is in pain, because pain and cough often travel together in older dogs.

Prevention worth doing

  • Vaccination. Kennel cough vaccines reduce severity even when they do not fully block infection, and are worth discussing if your dog boards, shows or attends daycare.
  • Avoid neck leads in small breeds and in any dog with a collapsing trachea.
  • Keep the home air clean, and be alert to grass seed season in late summer.
  • Keep body condition lean, and keep up the routine checks covered in our senior dog care guide once your dog passes seven.

When to see a vet

A cough that lasts more than a few days, gets worse, is wet or blood-tinged, or comes with laboured breathing, blue gums, fainting, fever or a dog that will not eat is a same-day vet visit - and a sudden, violent cough that started in one moment is an emergency, because it may be an object stuck in the airway.

The bottom line

Most mild coughs in a bright, eating dog settle within one to two weeks with rest, a harness instead of a neck collar and clean, smoke-free air - but book a vet visit the same day if the cough is wet, blood-tinged, lasts more than a few days, or comes with laboured breathing, fainting, fever or a dog that will not eat. A sudden, violent cough that started in one moment is an emergency, because it may be something stuck in the airway.

FAQs

How long is too long for a dog cough?

A mild cough in a bright, eating dog often settles within one to two weeks. Anything lasting more than a few days, getting worse, or lasting beyond two weeks needs a vet visit. If the cough is wet, blood-tinged or comes with laboured breathing, fainting or a dog that will not eat, do not wait at all - book the same day.

Can I give my dog human cough syrup?

No. Many human cough medicines contain paracetamol, decongestants or other ingredients that are toxic to dogs, and dosing by owner guesswork causes real harm. Keep your dog comfortable with rest, a harness instead of a collar and clean air, and let your vet choose any medication.

What does kennel cough sound like?

Usually a harsh, honking cough that ends with a retch or a foamy spit, often worse after excitement, drinking or exercise. It spreads easily wherever dogs mix. Healthy adult dogs often recover with rest and supportive care, but puppies, seniors and unwell dogs should be seen because it can progress.

Is a dog cough ever an emergency?

Yes. A sudden, violent, repeated cough that started in one moment may mean an object or grass seed is stuck in the airway. Blue gums, gasping, fainting, collapse, or breathing that is fast and laboured at rest are also emergencies. Go straight to a vet rather than waiting to see if it settles.
