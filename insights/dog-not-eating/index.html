Why Is My Dog Not Eating? Causes and When to Call a Vet

General · 2026-10-10

Bottom line: A skipped meal in a bright, drinking, otherwise normal adult dog is usually nothing, and a little patience plus a bland, warm meal settles most cases within a day. A refusal that lasts past 24 hours, or that comes with vomiting, a painful belly, lethargy, weight loss, or a puppy or senior who will not eat, is a vet visit today - appetite loss is a symptom with a hundred causes, not a diagnosis.

A dog that skips a meal is usually just off their food, and most cases settle within 24 hours - but a refusal that lasts longer, comes with vomiting, a painful belly or weakness, or hits a puppy or a senior, needs a vet the same day. The useful question is never "did my dog eat?" on its own. It is how long it has lasted, what changed around it, and how your dog is behaving between meals.

Why appetite is a useful signal

Appetite is one of the earliest things to change when a dog is unwell, which makes it a genuinely useful warning light - and a noisy one, because healthy dogs go off their food all the time. A dog that turns its nose up at breakfast but is still wagging, drinking, walking and toileting normally is usually fine. A dog that stops eating *and* stops being itself is telling you something different. Watch the whole dog, not just the bowl.

How long is too long without food

  • One skipped meal, normal behaviour: almost never a problem. Offer the next meal as usual.
  • 12 to 24 hours with normal energy and drinking: keep watching. Tempt with a small amount of something warm and appealing, and call your vet if the next meal also goes untouched.
  • More than 24 hours without eating: call your vet. Dogs are not designed to fast for long, and a day without food in a small breed, a puppy or a senior can turn a mild problem into a serious one.
  • Any age, any refusal alongside other signs: vomiting, diarrhoea, a hard or swollen belly, weakness, shivering, a hunched back or obvious pain means the same-day call, not a wait-and-see.

The most common causes

1. A sudden diet change or a new food. Swapping food overnight is a classic trigger. If you must change, transition over 7 to 10 days, mixing old and new together.

2. Stress and household change. Boarding, a move, a new baby, building work or a new pet in the house. Our calm and behavior guide covers how to lower a stressed dog's arousal without forcing interaction.

3. Dental pain. A cracked tooth, a loose tooth or advanced gum disease makes chewing hurt, and a dog in mouth pain often drops food or eats on one side before it stops eating entirely. Our dog dental disease signs guide lists what to check at home.

4. Nausea and gut upset. Something scavenged, a fatty treat, a gut infection or a new medication can all kill appetite. If vomiting is part of the picture, our dog vomiting guide walks through what to do in the first 12 hours.

5. Pain somewhere else. Arthritis, back pain, an ear infection or an abscess makes a dog dull and off its food. Our 8 signs your dog is in pain is the fastest checklist for this.

6. Heat, dehydration and pain on swallowing. A dog that is too hot, or that has not drunk enough, will often refuse food before it refuses water. If drinking has dropped too, see our guide to a dog not drinking water.

7. Vaccination and medication. A day of feeling flat after a vaccine or a new tablet is common and usually short-lived.

8. Underlying disease. Kidney or liver disease, pancreatitis, diabetes, an obstruction, pyometra in an unspayed female, and some cancers all present with appetite loss. These are the cases where early diagnosis genuinely changes the outcome.

Checking your dog at home

  • Lift the lip and look at the gums. They should be pink and moist. Pale, white, blue or brick-red gums are a reason to call a vet now.
  • Gently press the skin between the shoulder blades. It should spring straight back. If it stays tented, your dog is dehydrated.
  • Take the temperature if you have a pet thermometer. Above roughly 39.4 degrees Celsius (103 F) is a fever.
  • Watch the toilet. Diarrhoea, black tarry stools, straining, or a sudden stop in urination all change the picture.
  • Weigh mentally. Ribs and spine that feel sharper than last month mean the appetite loss has been going on longer than you thought.

What to try tonight

1. Offer a small, warm, bland meal - plain boiled chicken with white rice, or a prescription bland diet. Warm food smells stronger and is more appealing.

2. Hand-feed a few pieces if your dog is anxious. Eating from your hand is low-pressure and tells you whether the problem is the food or the appetite.

3. Skip the walk and keep the house calm. Rest helps a queasy stomach.

4. Make sure water is fresh and within reach, and offer it often in small amounts.

5. Keep a short note of what your dog ate, when it last ate properly, and any vomiting or diarrhoea. It saves time at the appointment.

What not to do

  • Do not leave food down all day. Grazing makes it impossible to tell whether your dog is eating, and stale food loses its appeal.
  • Do not bribe with fatty table scraps or cheese. A rich meal can trigger pancreatitis and turn appetite loss into a hospital stay.
  • Do not fast a puppy, a toy breed or a senior dog. They have no reserves and dehydrate fast.
  • Do not force-feed. It risks choking and aspiration, and it hides the problem.
  • Do not give human medication for nausea, pain or appetite. Ibuprofen, paracetamol and many others are toxic to dogs.
  • Do not use an appetite stimulant from a pet shop to mask a cause you have not identified.

When to see a vet

Call your vet the same day if your dog has not eaten for more than 24 hours, if it has stopped eating altogether after eating normally, or if the refusal comes with vomiting, diarrhoea, a swollen or painful belly, lethargy, pale gums, weight loss, bad breath with drooling, or a fever. Puppies, small breeds, seniors, pregnant dogs and any dog on prescription medication move to the front of the queue. An unspayed female who stops eating and seems uncomfortable is an emergency, because pyometra can be fatal within days. Any suspected toxin or swallowed object is a call now, not a wait-and-see. A skipped meal in a bright, drinking, otherwise normal adult dog is usually nothing, and a little patience plus a bland, warm meal settles most cases within a day. A refusal that lasts past 24 hours, or that comes with vomiting, a painful belly, lethargy, weight loss, or a puppy or senior who will not eat, is a vet visit today - appetite loss is a symptom with a hundred causes, not a diagnosis.

FAQs

How long can a dog go without eating before it's a problem?

A healthy adult dog can miss a meal or even a day without harm, and a fussy day with normal energy and drinking is usually nothing. Past 24 hours without food, or any refusal in a puppy, toy breed, senior or dog on medication, it stops being a waiting game and becomes a call to your vet.

My dog won't eat but is still drinking and playful. Should I worry?

Usually not on day one. Bright, drinking, toileting and active dogs that skip a single meal are typically just off their food. Watch the next two meals, keep the food warm and small, and call your vet if a full day passes or if any other sign appears.

Should I change my dog's food if it stops eating?

Not immediately, and never by swapping overnight. A sudden change is itself a common cause of a fussy or upset stomach. Try warming the current food or adding a little water first, and if you do change, mix old and new over 7 to 10 days.

Can I give my dog something to boost its appetite?

Only with veterinary guidance. Appetite stimulants prescribed by a vet are safe and useful once the cause is known, but using shop products or human medication to push a dog to eat can mask a serious problem and delay diagnosis.
