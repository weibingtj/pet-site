Dog in Heat: How Long the Cycle Lasts and What to Do

General · 2026-10-30

Bottom line: Keep a dog in heat on the lead and away from entire males for the full three to four weeks, remember the fertile days arrive when the discharge lightens, and treat foul discharge, lethargy and increased thirst as a same-day vet visit.

A female dog's heat cycle (oestrus) lasts two to four weeks from start to finish and returns every six to twelve months depending on her size. The bloody discharge is only the first stage - the highest-risk days for an unplanned pregnancy come later, when the discharge lightens and she starts actively inviting males.

The four stages of the dog heat cycle

1. Proestrus (about 7 to 10 days). The vulva swells, a bloody discharge appears, and male dogs become intensely interested - but she will refuse mating at this stage. Most owners think this whole phase is "the heat" when it is really the warm-up.

2. Oestrus (about 9 days, anywhere from 3 to 21). The discharge lightens to pink or straw colour, she flag her tail to the side and will accept mating. This is the fertile window: eggs are released over several days, so pregnancies happen here, not during the red discharge days.

3. Dioestrus (about 2 months). Hormone levels stay high whether or not she is pregnant. Some dogs develop a false pregnancy in this phase - nesting, mothering toys, even producing milk.

4. Anoestrus. The quiet reproductive holiday between cycles, lasting months, until the whole thing starts again.

The practical takeaway: your dog is potentially fertile from roughly day 9 to day 14 of visible signs, but no calendar is reliable enough to plan around, and sperm can survive inside her for days after a single mating.

How often dogs come into heat

  • The first heat usually arrives between 6 and 24 months of age - small breeds earlier, giant breeds later
  • Small breeds often cycle every 6 to 8 months; large breeds every 8 to 12; some giant breeds only once a year
  • Cycles are often irregular in the first year before settling into a pattern
  • Unspayed females keep cycling into old age; dogs do not have a menopause

Managing a dog in heat at home

The single biggest job is pregnancy prevention, because a determined male will go through fences, screens and open doors:

  • Never leave her unattended outside, even in a fenced garden, for the entire three to four weeks
  • Walk on the lead only, at quiet times, and skip the dog park completely
  • Keep her fully separated from any entire male in the household - that includes shared rooms and car rides
  • Expect personality changes: clinginess, restlessness, more urine marking, some grumpiness
  • Washable britches protect furniture and carpets; change them daily and clean the vulva area gently
  • Watch for escape attempts at doors and windows, especially mid-cycle

None of this is punishment - it is four weeks of sensible management while her hormones run their course.

Spaying and common myths

Spaying removes the heat cycle entirely and prevents both pyometra (a life-threatening womb infection that most commonly strikes in the weeks after a heat) and most mammary tumours, which are among the commonest cancers in unspayed females. The ideal timing is worth a conversation with your own vet, since many now suggest letting larger breeds reach skeletal maturity first.

Two myths to drop:

  • "Let her have one litter first." There is no medical or behavioural benefit to this, and it only adds pregnancy risk
  • "She is bleeding, so she must be off her heat by now." Lighter discharge actually means she is entering her most fertile days, not leaving them

If a spayed female ever shows bloody discharge, that is not a leftover heat - it points to a urinary infection, incontinence or something else that needs an exam.

When to see a vet

Call the vet the same day if the discharge turns foul or pus-like, or your dog is lethargic, off food and drinking noticeably more - that combination can signal pyometra, a surgical emergency that typically appears in the weeks after a heat. Book promptly if a heat runs longer than about three weeks, if bleeding seems very heavy, if she is straining to urinate, or if any suspected mating happened despite your precautions. A spayed dog with bloody discharge also always needs an appointment, since it is never a normal heat.

FAQs

How long does a dog stay in heat?

The visible signs usually run about two to three weeks, with a total cycle window of up to four weeks once you count the fertile tail end. Swelling and discharge fade before fertility is fully over, so the safe period for separation is the whole three to four weeks, not just the bleeding stage.

When can my dog get pregnant during her cycle?

During oestrus, the middle stage that begins when the red discharge lightens and she starts accepting males - typically around days 9 to 14. Because eggs release over several days and sperm survive for days after a single mating, there is no completely safe day to relax supervision.

Can a dog in heat go for walks?

Yes, on a lead, at quiet times, avoiding dog parks and any area where entire males roam. Walks keep her calm and exercised, but she must never be off-lead or unsupervised in the garden, because a male in scent range will find her.

Do dog diapers prevent pregnancy?

No - they are a hygiene product, not contraception, and a determined male will get one off or around it in seconds. Diapers are useful for protecting furniture and keeping her clean, but supervision and physical separation are the only things that actually prevent mating.
