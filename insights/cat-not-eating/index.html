Cat Not Eating: When to Worry and What to Try First

General · 2026-10-18

Bottom line: Time the fast before you do anything else: 24 hours without food means a same-day vet call, 48 hours means an emergency, and kittens or jaundiced cats go now - in the meantime warm the food, offer a strong-smelling wet option in a quiet spot, and never force-feed.

A cat that has eaten nothing for 24 hours needs a vet call today, and 48 hours without food is an emergency - not because cats are dramatic, but because their livers are not built to fast. When intake drops, cats mobilise body fat quickly, and that fat overwhelms the liver. The result is hepatic lipidosis, or fatty liver, which can begin within two to three days, especially in an overweight cat.

So the first question is not "what can I tempt her with" but "how long has it actually been". Picking at biscuits or licking gravy buys a little time. Total anorexia does not.

Why cats handle fasting badly

Dogs can go a couple of days without food and usually be fine. Cats cannot. Their metabolism is geared to frequent small meals, and the liver is the weak link. Once fat stores start moving into the liver faster than it can process them, the liver swells and stops working properly. The cruel part is that the sicker the cat gets, the less she wants to eat, so the problem feeds itself.

Cats with excess body fat, middle-aged to older cats, and cats already ill with something else are at the highest risk. A thin, healthy two-year-old has more slack than a chunky ten-year-old.

First, confirm it is really nothing

Owners are often wrong about this, in both directions. Before you panic or relax, check:

  • Weigh the food. Put a set amount down and weigh what is left after 24 hours. A cat eating 20 grams a day is not eating, even if the bowl looks touched.
  • Check for other food sources. A neighbour who feeds her, a bin, a housemate topping up the bowl, hunting.
  • Look at the litter tray. Small, hard stools and concentrated urine suggest reduced intake for a while.
  • Watch the water bowl. A cat drinking more while eating less points towards kidney disease, diabetes or hyperthyroidism.

The common causes, in rough order

  • Mouth pain. Dental disease, a broken tooth, stomatitis or tooth resorption makes eating hurt. Cats often still approach the bowl, then drop food or eat on one side. See cat stomatitis and cat tooth resorption.
  • A blocked nose. A cat decides most of what food is by smell. Cat flu or chronic rhinitis shuts that sense down and food becomes uninteresting rather than painful.
  • Stress and change. A new pet, a move, a new food, building work, or a bowl placed next to a noisy appliance.
  • Gut problems. Hairballs, foreign bodies (string, tinsel, foam), pancreatitis, inflammatory bowel disease.
  • Systemic illness. Chronic kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, diabetes, cancer, fever or pain anywhere.
  • Medication. Some antibiotics and painkillers suppress appetite, and a cat that feels nauseous will not eat no matter how good the food is.

What you can safely try in the first 24 hours

1. Warm the food to body temperature. Warm food releases far more aroma than cold food straight from the fridge.

2. Offer strong-smelling options. A pouch of strong fishy food, or a small amount of warm poached chicken, is a reasonable test. Keep it small so you are not throwing away bowls of food.

3. Change the texture and the dish. Some cats refuse kibble with sore gums but will lap a pate or a liquid treat. Wide, shallow dishes stop whiskers touching the sides - see the feeder range in our shop.

4. Move the bowl somewhere quiet, away from the litter tray, the dog and foot traffic. Feed little and often.

5. Try a food puzzle or hand-feeding for a cat that is well but bored.

If a cat is nauseous, all of this fails. That is useful information, not a failure on your part - anti-nausea medication from your vet often restarts eating within hours.

What not to do

  • Do not force-feed. Syringing food into a cat's mouth risks aspiration into the lungs and creates a food aversion that outlasts the illness.
  • Do not use milk. Most adult cats are lactose intolerant and it causes diarrhoea, which makes dehydration worse.
  • Do not run a tuna-only diet for more than a day or two. It is nutritionally incomplete and cats imprint on it fast.
  • Do not wait it out on a fat cat. Body condition is the single biggest risk factor for fatty liver, and those cats need earlier intervention, not later.

How long is too long

Use these cut-offs. They are deliberately conservative because the downside of waiting is severe.

  • 24 hours of no food in an adult cat: call your vet for advice the same day.
  • 12 hours in a kitten, or in any cat that is also vomiting, lethargic, hiding or breathing oddly: go now.
  • 48 hours in any cat: emergency appointment.
  • Any cat showing jaundice - yellow gums, ears or the whites of the eyes - is already in liver trouble.

A cat that is eating a little but losing weight over weeks is a different case. That one needs bloodwork and a dental check, not an emergency, but it should not be left for months. Dogs follow different rules, so if you are dealing with a dog read dog not eating instead.

When to see a vet: call immediately if your cat has eaten nothing for 24 hours, is vomiting or hiding, has yellow gums or eyes, or is a kitten that has missed even two meals - do not try home remedies first.

The bottom line

Time the fast before you do anything else: 24 hours without food means a same-day vet call, 48 hours means an emergency, and kittens or jaundiced cats go now - in the meantime warm the food, offer a strong-smelling wet option in a quiet spot, and never force-feed.

Sources: Cornell University College of Veterinary Medicine - Feline Hepatic Lipidosis; VCA Animal Hospitals - Anorexia in Cats; AAHA - Feline Life Stage and Nutrition Guidelines.

FAQs

How long can a cat go without eating before it is dangerous?

A healthy adult cat should not go more than 24 hours with zero food before you call a vet. By 48 hours the risk of hepatic lipidosis rises sharply, and overweight cats can develop it in two to three days. Kittens have almost no reserves, so 12 hours without food is already a reason to be seen.

Will a cat starve itself on purpose?

Rarely out of stubbornness. Cats refuse food because of pain, nausea, a blocked nose or stress, and a hunger strike lasting more than a day usually means something physical. A cat that refuses food but drinks and behaves normally still needs a vet check within 24 hours, because dental pain and kidney disease both start this way.

What is the best food to tempt a sick cat with?

Warm, strong-smelling wet food at body temperature, offered in a small amount in a quiet place. poached chicken or a liquid treat works as a short-term test. If she refuses even that and is also lethargic or vomiting, stop tempting and book an appointment - nausea needs medication, not a better flavour.

Can I syringe-feed my cat if she will not eat?

No. Force-feeding by syringe risks food going into the airway and causing aspiration pneumonia, and it can create a lasting aversion to being handled at mealtimes. Assisted feeding is sometimes appropriate, but it should be set up and demonstrated by your vet, with the right consistency and volume.
