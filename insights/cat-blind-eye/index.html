Why Is My Cat Going Blind? Signs, Causes and Next Steps

Eye Care & Tear Stains · 2026-10-30

Bottom line: A cat going blind needs a vet visit within days - sudden bumping, fixed dilated pupils or blood visible inside the eye mean same-day care, because blood pressure treatment can still save part of a cat's vision.

Blindness in cats usually develops gradually, and cats hide vision loss so well that many owners only notice when furniture is rearranged or the cat stops jumping. The two most common causes are high blood pressure and retinal disease, and sudden onset is a veterinary emergency, because treatment started within days can sometimes restore partial vision.

Signs your cat is losing vision

Vision loss rarely announces itself with obvious clumsiness at home, where a cat can navigate from memory. Look for these patterns instead:

  • Bumping into walls or furniture, especially in unfamiliar spaces
  • Reluctance to jump up or down, or badly misjudging jumps she used to make
  • Pupils that stay widely dilated even in bright light
  • A low, crouched walking posture with whiskers swept forward as feelers
  • Claws catching in carpets and rugs because the gait has changed
  • Startling easily when approached from behind or touched without warning
  • Becoming unusually clingy and vocal, or the opposite, hiding away
  • Eyes that look cloudy, red, or show new visible blood vessels

The main causes of feline blindness

1. High blood pressure (hypertension). This is the leading cause of sudden blindness in older cats. Persistently raised pressure damages the delicate retinal vessels, causing bleeding or detachment. Most hypertensive cats have underlying kidney disease or an overactive thyroid, so any blind cat's workup includes a blood pressure measurement and blood tests, not just an eye exam.

2. Retinal detachment. Often the direct result of hypertension, sometimes of inflammation or trauma. The retina floats off the back of the eye and stops working; the speed of reattachment largely decides whether vision returns.

3. Glaucoma and uveitis. Raised pressure inside the eyeball or chronic inflammation inside the eye quietly destroys retinal tissue. These are the conditions a vet rules out first in any painful, red or cloudy eye.

4. Cataracts. Less common in cats than in dogs, and usually tied to diabetes or long-standing eye inflammation rather than simple ageing.

5. Taurine deficiency. Rare on complete commercial diets, but still seen in cats fed unbalanced home-prepared or all-meat-without-supplement rations. Taurine deficiency also damages the heart.

6. Tumours and trauma. Cancer inside the eye, or a head injury, can destroy vision in one eye quickly.

Can a blind cat's vision come back?

It depends entirely on the cause and how fast treatment starts. Retinal detachment driven by hypertension can partially recover when blood pressure is brought down within days, which is exactly why sudden signs deserve a same-day appointment rather than watchful waiting. Damage from chronic glaucoma or a long-standing detached retina is usually permanent. Even when vision cannot be saved, treating the underlying disease still matters enormously: it protects the kidneys and heart and prevents the same disaster in the remaining good eye.

How to help a blind cat at home

Cats compensate remarkably well using hearing, smell, memory and whiskers, and a blind indoor cat can have an excellent quality of life. Your job is to make the home predictable:

  • Keep furniture, litter trays and food bowls exactly where they are - a blind cat navigates by mental map, and moving things resets it
  • Block access to open stair tops, balconies and open windows
  • Talk or tap gently before touching so you never startle her
  • Use consistent scent cues: the same bed, the same bowl placement
  • Keep her strictly indoors, or allow supervised time in a secure catio
  • Swap silent toys for ones that crinkle, squeak or jingle

When to see a vet

Book a same-day appointment if your cat suddenly starts bumping into things, has widely dilated pupils that do not respond to light, or you can see blood inside the eye - these are the classic signs of hypertensive retinal detachment and hours count. Go within 24 hours for any red, cloudy or painful eye, and promptly for increased thirst, weight loss or a racing heartbeat, which point to the underlying kidney or thyroid disease. Even a slowly worsening eye this week still needs an exam this week, not next month.

An eye exam plus a blood pressure check takes minutes and can genuinely save sight. FAQs

Can a blind cat live a happy life?

Yes, and usually a very good one. Cats lean on hearing, smell, whiskers and memory far more than sight, so a blind indoor cat that lives in a predictable, uncluttered home typically eats, plays and grooms normally within weeks of losing vision.

What are the first signs a cat is going blind?

Night vision usually fades first, so the earliest clues are hesitating at dusk, refusing to jump in low light, and dilated pupils that linger even in daylight. Many owners first notice something is wrong only when the cat bumps into furniture in a familiar room.

Does high blood pressure cause blindness in cats?

It is the single most common cause of sudden blindness in older cats. Persistent hypertension makes the retinal vessels leak or the retina detach, and it almost always traces back to kidney disease or an overactive thyroid, which is why a blind cat gets blood tests as well as an eye exam.

How do vets test a cat's vision?

They check the menace response (blinking when a hand approaches), follow objects, navigate an obstacle course, and examine the eyes and pupils with lights, then measure blood pressure and screen blood for the systemic diseases behind most feline blindness.
