How to Deshed Your Dog and Cut Loose Fur at Home

Skin & Coat · 2026-09-29

Bottom line: Shedding is normal, but loose fur everywhere is not inevitable. A vet-informed deshedding routine — tools, bath timing and diet — that actually reduces the fur on your floor.

Shedding is a process, not a problem

Every dog with a double coat sheds; it is how the old undercoat makes room for the new. You cannot stop shedding, but you can cut the fur on your floor by 70–90% with a consistent deshedding routine. The trick is removing loose undercoat before it lands on the sofa — not after.

The tools that actually work

  • Undercoat rake or de-shedding tool — reaches the soft underlayer a normal brush misses; use a few light passes, not a scrub.
  • Slicker brush — for the topcoat and tangles, everyday maintenance.
  • Rubber curry — great on short coats and in the bath to lift loose hair.
  • High-velocity dryer — the pro secret; blows out loose undercoat fast after a bath.

A deshedding routine that sticks

1. Brush before the bath — loosen and lift the undercoat dry so water does not mat it.

2. Bath with a pH-balanced shampoo — clean coat releases hair better; a fur serum after helps.

3. Dry and blast — towel, then a dryer on low; the moving air carries out the loose undercoat.

4. Final pass — a few minutes with the de-shedding tool catches what remains.

Most homes see a real drop in fur within one to two sessions a week. Consistency beats a heroic monthly marathon.

Bathing timing matters

Too much bathing dries the skin and can increase shedding from irritation; too little lets loose coat build. For most healthy dogs, every 4–6 weeks is the sweet spot, with waterless foam between baths. A leave-in serum with panthenol and glycerin after the bath keeps the coat from breaking.

Diet and hydration play a quiet role

A coat is built from protein, omega-3s and water. Dogs on a balanced diet with fish-oil support tend to shed a finer, less-volume coat; dehydration shows as dry, brittle fur that breaks and scatters. It will not stop shedding, but it changes how much you notice.

Don't fall for the common myths

  • Myth: shaving stops shedding. It only hides it for a shaved dog; the undercoat grows back and double-coated breeds can shed more oddly after.
  • Myth: daily hard brushing is best. Over-brushing irritates skin and raises shedding from damage; light, frequent passes win.
  • Myth: supplements cure shedding. They support coat quality, not the biological cycle — manage expectations.
When to see a vet: see your vet if shedding comes with bald patches, red or irritated skin, constant scratching, or thinning coat outside the normal seasonal blowout. Sudden heavy loss, especially in clumps, can signal infection, parasites, allergies or hormone issues — not just shedding.

The line we won't cross

This is education, not a diagnosis. We would rather you rule out a medical cause than blame the brush. Our shop carries the deshedding and coat tools; the vet carries the answer when shedding looks wrong.

The bottom line

Shedding is normal, but loose fur everywhere is not inevitable. A vet-informed deshedding routine — tools, bath timing and diet — that actually reduces the fur on your floor.

FAQs

How often should I deshed my dog?

For most double-coated dogs, one to two sessions a week keeps loose fur off your floor; short-coated dogs need less. Brush before baths and do a final pass after drying. Light, frequent passes beat a long monthly scrub.

Does shaving a dog stop shedding?

No. Shaving hides shedding on that dog but does not stop the biological cycle, and double-coated breeds can regrow coat unevenly. Deshedding tools remove the loose undercoat without the risks of shaving.

Why is my dog shedding in clumps?

Seasonal blowout is normal for double coats, but clumps with bald patches, red skin or scratching are not — those point to infection, parasites, allergies or hormone problems and need a vet.

Will a better diet reduce shedding?

Diet and hydration improve coat quality and can lower visible breakage, but they do not stop the natural shed cycle. A balanced diet with omega-3 support makes the fur finer and less scattered, not absent.
