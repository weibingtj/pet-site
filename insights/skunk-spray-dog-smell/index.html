Skunk Spray on a Dog: What Actually Removes the Smell

Grooming, Paws & Ears · 2026-11-14

Flush the eyes first, keep peroxide off the face below the eyes, and get to a vet if the dog is squinting, vomiting, pale or wobbly. A spare towel and a bottle of de-skunk shampoo by the back door turn the next encounter into twenty minutes instead of a weekend.

Skunk spray is a blend of thiols — sulfur compounds that latch onto skin, hair and fabric — so ordinary shampoo mostly spreads it around. The mix that works is 3% hydrogen peroxide, baking soda and a squirt of liquid dish soap, applied to a dry coat, left for about five minutes, then rinsed. Tomato juice just covers the smell with something worse.

Why skunk spray is so hard to remove

Two things make it stubborn. First, the smell comes from thiols, the same family of chemicals that gives garlic and burnt rubber their staying power — they bind to protein in hair and skin rather than sitting on the surface. Second, skunk oil is, literally, an oil: it spreads through the coat and soaks into collars, harnesses and the dog bed.

Peroxide oxidises thiols into compounds that have no smell. Baking soda raises the pH so the peroxide works faster. Dish soap lifts the oil so the whole thing can be rinsed away. That is the entire logic of the recipe, and it is why water alone does nothing.

The de-skunking mix

Mix this fresh, in a plastic bucket, immediately before use:

  • 1 quart (about 1 litre) of 3% hydrogen peroxide
  • 1/4 cup baking soda
  • 1 teaspoon liquid dish soap

Three warnings matter more than the recipe:

  • Never store it in a closed container. It off-gasses and can burst a bottle. Make it, use it, dump the rest.
  • Keep it away from the eyes and out of the mouth. Peroxide in an eye is a chemical injury.
  • Do not leave it on for longer than about five minutes. Peroxide can lighten a dark coat and irritate skin, so treat it as a one-off rescue, not a shampoo.

If your dog has a dark or black coat and you would rather not risk lightening, ask your vet about a commercial oxidising de-skunk shampoo — same chemistry, less guesswork.

Step by step

1. Keep the dog outside. Every minute indoors before treatment means skunk oil on soft furnishings.

2. Check the eyes and mouth first. Flush eyes with plain water or saline for 10 to 15 minutes before you do anything else. If the dog is drooling, retching or pawing at its face, call your vet.

3. Put on gloves and old clothes. You will smell like this too.

4. Wet the coat thoroughly with warm water, then work the mixture through the fur — armpits, groin, between the toes, under the chin, the base of the tail. A sponge helps on the head.

5. Wait about five minutes. Keep the dog from licking; a cone or a helper with a treat works.

6. Rinse and rinse again, then wash with a normal dog shampoo to remove residue. Repeat the whole mix once if the smell is still sharp.

7. Do the face separately with a damp cloth and plain dog shampoo. No peroxide above the eyes.

8. Wash the collar and harness, and hot-wash the bedding and any towel that touched the dog.

The tomato juice myth and other dead ends

  • Tomato juice does not neutralise thiols. It is acidic, it stains light coats, and it leaves you with a dog that smells of tomato and skunk.
  • Vinegar is a decent final rinse to cut residue, but on its own it will not touch a direct hit.
  • Perfume, air freshener, fabric spray add a second smell on top of the first. Now you have two problems.
  • Bleach, or any harsh cleaner on the skin is a burn waiting to happen.
  • Waiting for it to fade takes weeks to months, and every soft surface in the house joins in.

A weak vinegar or diluted mouthwash rinse (one part to four parts water) after the peroxide step genuinely helps with the last lingering trace, especially on the head and neck where you did not dare use peroxide.

Eyes, mouth and the first hour

Skunk spray in the eyes causes heavy tearing, squinting and redness. Flush with water or saline for a full 10 to 15 minutes, then keep the dog from rubbing its face. If the eye stays shut, looks cloudy, or the dog is still squinting an hour later, that is a vet visit — corneal irritation after a direct hit is common.

Spray in the mouth brings drooling, gagging and sometimes vomiting. Offer water, do not induce vomiting, and call your vet or a poison helpline if the dog seems unwell.

A rare reaction worth knowing about

Heavy, close-range exposure — usually a dog that takes a direct hit in the face — can cause skunk toxicosis: trembling, weakness, vomiting, pale or muddy gums, and in severe cases damage to red blood cells. It is uncommon, but it is the reason a dog that looks wobbly or has changed gum colour after a spraying goes to the vet rather than the bathtub.

When to see a vet

Call your vet if the eyes are swollen shut or still squinting after 15 minutes of flushing, if there is vomiting, drooling or refusal to eat, if the gums look pale or grey, or if the dog is weak, wobbly or trembling. Also call if a very young, elderly or pregnant dog takes a direct facial hit. For everything else — a smelly but otherwise bright, eating, drinking dog — the peroxide mix and a warm bath are the right first move.

The bottom line

FAQs

Does tomato juice really get rid of skunk smell?

No. It masks the smell temporarily and stains light coats. The smell comes from sulfur compounds that need to be oxidised, which is what hydrogen peroxide does. Tomato juice cannot do that chemistry.

Can I use the peroxide mix on a puppy or a cat?

Ask your vet before using it on a puppy under about eight weeks or on any cat. Cats are far more sensitive to many topical products, and young puppies have thinner skin. For a cat, call your vet first and use a species-appropriate shampoo in the meantime.

How long does skunk smell last on a dog?

Untreated, it can linger for weeks to months and will keep transferring to bedding and furniture. After one proper peroxide treatment most dogs are dramatically better, with a faint trace around the head and neck for a few days. A second treatment 24 hours later usually finishes it.

Will the peroxide bleach my dog's coat?

It can lighten dark coats slightly, especially if left on too long. Keep it to five minutes, rinse thoroughly, and avoid the face. If your dog is black or dark brown and you want zero risk, ask your vet for a commercial oxidising de-skunk shampoo instead.
