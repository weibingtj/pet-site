Cat Watery Eye: Causes, Discharge & When to See a Vet

A watery eye in a cat is almost always tear overflow - tears spilling over the lid because the surface is irritated or the drainage channel is partly blocked - and it is not a sign of sadness. If the eye is wide open, the discharge is clear and your cat is eating and behaving normally, clean it and watch for 24 hours. If the eye is squinting, cloudy, red or swollen, or your cat is pawing at the face, that needs a vet today.

What a watery eye actually means

Cats do not cry from emotion. Tears are produced continuously by the lacrimal gland and by glands hidden behind the third eyelid, and they do three jobs: they carry oxygen and nutrients to the cornea, they wash away dust and debris, and they carry antibodies and immune cells to the surface of the eye. The surplus normally drains through a small opening at the inner corner, down a duct that runs through the skull and empties into the nose.

A watery eye means that balance has tipped. Either production has risen, or drainage has slowed - or both at once. So the useful question is not "why is my cat sad" but "is this too much going in, or too little getting out".

Too much in: irritation and inflammation

Something is bothering the surface of the eye, so it floods itself in response.

  • Conjunctivitis. The pink tissue lining the lids becomes inflamed. Feline herpesvirus is a very common cause, and it tends to flare during stress, illness or a change in the household. Expect redness and swelling alongside the tears.
  • Corneal irritation. A scratch from another cat, a foreign body such as a grass seed or grit, an eyelash growing the wrong way, or simply very dry air.
  • Allergy and irritants. Dust, tobacco smoke, aerosol sprays, scented litter, or a new household cleaner.
  • Upper respiratory infection. A watery eye often travels with sneezing and a runny nose, especially in kittens and cats recently out of a shelter. This is the herpesvirus and calicivirus combination people call cat flu.
  • Something lodged inside the eye. A foreign body can sit under the third eyelid where you cannot see it without lifting the lid.

Too little out: the drainage problem

When the duct that carries tears to the nose narrows or blocks, tears pool at the inner corner and spill down the face. The classic picture is a permanently damp, rust-coloured stain in the fur under the eye with no redness and no squinting - the eye itself looks comfortable. Common reasons include:

  • Scarring of the duct after a bout of cat flu, which is one of the most frequent causes in adult cats.
  • Anatomical narrowing, which is common in flat-faced breeds such as Persians and Exotic Shorthairs.
  • A duct that never opened properly, which shows up in kittens.
  • Blockage by debris or inflammation, which is sometimes reversible with a flush.

What the discharge tells you

Colour and texture are useful clues, though they do not replace an examination.

  • Clear and watery with a bright, wide-open eye usually means mild irritation, an allergy, or a partially blocked duct.
  • Sticky grey or white discharge suggests a low-grade infection or conjunctivitis that is settling.
  • Thick yellow or green discharge with a red, swollen eye points to bacterial infection and needs treatment.
  • Rust-coloured fur only, with no redness and no squinting, is tear staining rather than active eye disease.

Safe home care while you decide

1. Wipe with a clean, damp pad. Warm water on a cotton pad, one fresh pad per eye, wiping gently downwards and outwards away from the eye. Never wipe back towards the tear duct, where you would push debris in.

2. Keep the fur dry afterwards. Constant moisture grows yeast and bacteria and makes staining worse. Pat dry, then leave the area alone.

3. Cut down on irritants. Switch from scented clay litter to a low-dust option, stop using aerosols in the same room, and keep your cat out of smoky spaces.

4. Do not use human eye drops or leftover pet drops. Preservative-containing human drops sting, and any drop containing a steroid can worsen a corneal ulcer or an active herpes flare.

5. Do not use a cotton bud on the eye itself. You cannot see what you are doing, and the cornea scratches easily.

When to see a vet

Book a same-day appointment if the eye is squinting or held closed, if the surface looks cloudy or bluish, if the eye is red, bulging or clearly swollen, if the discharge is thick and yellow or green, if the third eyelid slides across the eye, or if your cat is pawing at the face, hiding, off food or sneezing heavily. Corneal ulcers, herpes flare-ups and blocked tear ducts all need a proper examination, and a corneal ulcer can worsen within a day, so do not wait to see whether it settles on its own. This article is general education and not veterinary advice, and it cannot diagnose your cat.

What your vet will check

A full eye examination takes a few minutes but looks at things you cannot see at home.

  • Fluorescein stain - an orange dye that turns green where the corneal surface is damaged, which is how ulcers are found.
  • Schirmer tear test - a small paper strip that measures tear production, to rule out a dry eye that is paradoxically causing overflow.
  • Eyelid and third-eyelid inspection - looking for a rolled lid, an ingrown hair or a trapped foreign body.
  • Tear duct flush - passing saline through the duct to see whether it reaches the nose.
  • Pressure and retinal check - for glaucoma and for changes deeper inside the eye.

The bottom line

A clear watery eye on a comfortable, wide-open eye can usually be cleaned and watched for a day, but squinting, cloudiness, redness or coloured discharge means a same-day appointment - and never put a leftover human or steroid eye drop into a cat's eye. FAQs

Is a watery eye in cats a sign they are sad?

No. Cats do not produce emotional tears the way humans do. A watery eye means the tear film is overflowing the lid margin, which happens when the eye surface is irritated or the drainage duct to the nose is narrowed or blocked. Sadness or stress can contribute indirectly by suppressing the immune system and letting herpesvirus flare, but the tears themselves are a mechanical overflow, not an emotion.

How long can I safely watch a watery cat eye at home?

About 24 hours, and only if the eye is wide open, the discharge is clear or very slightly sticky, and your cat is eating, playing and behaving normally. The moment you see squinting, the eye held shut, cloudiness, redness or yellow-green discharge, stop watching and book an appointment. A corneal ulcer can go from mild to serious inside a day.

Can I use human eye drops on my cat?

Not without veterinary advice. Many human drops contain preservatives that sting and irritate a cat's cornea, and any product containing a steroid can make a corneal ulcer dramatically worse or let a herpesvirus infection run wild. Saline-only drops are less harmful but do nothing about the underlying cause. Clean the eye with warm water and let your vet choose any medication.

Why does the fur under my cat's eye look rusty brown?

That is tear staining. The porphyrins in tears oxidise when they sit in the fur and leave a reddish-brown mark, and the constant moisture also encourages yeast. It usually means the tear duct is draining poorly rather than that the eye is infected, especially if the eye is wide open and comfortable. Keep the area dry, treat any underlying cause, and ask your vet whether a duct flush is worth doing.
